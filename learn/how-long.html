How long things stay on your credit report

Last updated October 22, 2026 · Checked against official sources

The quick answer

Most negative items, like late payments, collections and charge offs, can stay on your credit report for seven years. For collections and charge offs, the seven years start 180 days after you first fell behind. Bankruptcy can stay for ten (15 U.S.C. 1681c). Hard credit checks stay for two years. When the time is up, the item should come off on its own, and if it does not, you can dispute it.

Late payment7 yearsThe date of the late payment
Collection account7 years180 days after you first fell behind on that debt
Charge off7 years180 days after you first fell behind on that debt
BankruptcyUp to 10 yearsThe date the case was filed
Hard credit check2 years on your report, and FICO scores only count it for 1The date of the check
Medical collectionNot shown if it is paid, under $500 or less than a year oldNot reported until it is a year old

What does not restart the clock

Paying a debt, disputing it, or a new collector buying it does not restart the seven years. The date that counts is when you first fell behind with the original company.

If a collection shows a newer date than your first missed payment, it can stay on your report too long. That is a mistake you can dispute.

Credit report time is not lawsuit time

How long something stays on your report is set by federal law. How long a collector can sue you is set by your state, and the two are different. In some states, paying or promising to pay an old debt can restart the time to sue. Read your state's rules before you pay a very old debt.

Can it come off sooner?

  • If it is wrong, dispute it. See the step by step guide.
  • If it is a late payment that really happened, try a goodwill letter.
  • If it is a collection, ask for a pay for delete.

Common questions

Does paying a debt reset the seven years?

No. The clock is based on when you first fell behind, and paying does not change that date.

Do old items still hurt my score?

Less over time. Older items count less than new ones, even before they come off.

What if an item is past its time and still there?

Dispute it with each credit bureau that shows it and point to the date you first fell behind.
